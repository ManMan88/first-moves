## The idea

Ever played something great, and then realised you weren't recording? On Move, that idea isn't lost. In Note Mode, Move is always quietly listening to what you play on the pads. Press **Capture** afterwards, and it turns your last bit of playing into a clip. That's **[Capture](/first-moves/glossary/#capture)**.

It's the perfect tool for noodling. You don't have to count in, or even decide on a speed first. Just play until something sounds good, then press Capture.

If Move was stopped when you capture, it also works out the **tempo** you were playing at, and sets the whole Set to match. It even offers three guesses, because the same groove can often be counted in more than one way, such as at double or half the speed. Your notes play back just the way you played them, whichever guess you choose.

Move treats the first note you played as the start of the loop, and every note you played goes inside it. So begin your phrase right on the first hit.

Once a clip is playing, Capture works for overdubbing too: jam along, press Capture, and the new notes join the clip. (Recording with the **Record** button is covered in [Lesson 11](/first-moves/lessons/11-record-live/).)

Because Move keeps everything you play, it also keeps your mistakes and your searching around. So you'll also learn how to wipe its memory before the take you want to keep.

<Steps>
  <Step press="note-session" see="Session Mode: each row of pads is one track, and each pad in a row is a place for a clip.">
    Make sure Move is stopped. Then press **Note/Session** to switch to Session Mode, so you can pick an empty spot for your idea.
    <Tip>If music is playing, press **Play** to stop it first. The tempo guessing only happens when Move is stopped.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="pad-1-1" see="The pad you pressed turns white: an empty clip slot is selected.">
    Press a **dark pad** in the row that belongs to your drum track. In a brand-new Set, the drums are on the top track. The drawing marks one example pad; any dark pad in that row will do.
    <Tip>Pads lit in colour already hold clips. Choose a dark pad so your capture becomes a new clip.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="track-1" see="Note Mode opens on the drum track. The pads play drum sounds.">
    Press the **track button** next to that row to open Note Mode on the drum track.
    <Tip>If the pads play notes of a melody, you're on a melodic track. Demo Sets and swapped sounds can differ from a new Set. Find the track that sounds like drums, and pick an empty slot in its row.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="drum-pads" hear="Different drum hits, one pad at a time.">
    Tap the pads in the left block and find three sounds by ear: a deep **kick**, a sharp **snare** and a short, ticking **hi-hat**. Remember where they are.
    <Tip>[Lesson 9](/first-moves/lessons/09-first-beat/) has tips for telling the drum sounds apart.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="shift capture" hold="shift" see="The display shows a message that the capture input was cleared.">
    Hold **Shift** and press **Capture**. This wipes what Move heard while you were searching, so those taps can't end up in your clip.
    <Tip>Pressing a track button, or starting and then stopping playback, clears it too, but without a message. Either way, the Capture button goes dark once the memory is empty.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="drum-pads" hear="Just your own drumming. Nothing else plays, because Move is stopped.">
    Now play a simple beat with the kick and the snare, steadily, four or five times round: kick, snare, kick, snare. Start right on your first kick.
    <Tip>Keep a steady pace, a bit like walking. A steady beat gives Move a much better chance of guessing your tempo.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="capture" hear="Your beat comes back straight away as a loop, and it keeps playing.">
    Press **Capture**. Move makes a new clip from what you just played and starts playing it.
    <Tip>The display now shows the tempo Move picked, in BPM (beats per minute). It should match the speed you played at.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="wheel" hear="The notes play at the same speed, but the loop may get shorter or longer.">
    Right away, turn **the wheel** to try the other tempo guesses. Pick the one where the loop sounds right to you.
    <Tip>Unlike changing the tempo by hand, these guesses don't speed up or slow down your notes. They only change how Move counts them, which can change where the loop ends. If turning the wheel does nothing, just keep the tempo Move chose.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="drum-pads" hear="Hi-hat taps on top of your looping beat.">
    While the loop plays, tap the hi-hat you found a few times between the kicks and snares. Keep going for one or two rounds of the loop.
  </Step>
  <Step press="capture" hear="The hi-hat taps are now part of the loop.">
    Press **Capture** again. Because your clip is already playing, the new notes are added to it.
    <Tip>Don't like how it came out? Press **Undo**. It takes back the last thing you did.</Tip>
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Try it yourself

Go to your bass track, pick an empty slot in its row and stop the music. Hum a little tune in your head and play it on the pads, without worrying about timing. Press **Capture** when you like it, and try the tempo guesses until the loop feels right. Then do it again on another track: every idea you capture is a clip you can come back to.

## Good to know

- Capture also works while you play along to your clips, but Move only guesses the tempo when it was stopped. When music is already playing, the tempo stays as it is.
- Besides notes, Capture keeps knob movements. Turn a **knob** while a clip loops, then press **Capture**, and the sound changes are saved into the clip.
- You can still set the tempo by hand afterwards, as in [Lesson 10](/first-moves/lessons/10-tempo-metronome-groove/). That changes the playback speed of your notes.
