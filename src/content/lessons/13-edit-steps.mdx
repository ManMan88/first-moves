## The idea

When you put a note on a step, Move gives it standard settings. It plays at one strength, lasts a set time and starts exactly on the step. That is a fine start, but it can sound a bit stiff. Real players never hit every note the same way. Each note has four things you can change:

- **How hard it plays.** This is its [velocity](/first-moves/glossary/#velocity), which you met in [Lesson 6](/first-moves/lessons/06-playing-pads/). A hit that is louder than the ones around it is called an **accent**. Accents give a beat its bounce. A classic trick is to make every other hi-hat a little louder.
- **How long it lasts.** Short, clipped notes sound bouncy and tight. Long notes that run into each other sound smooth and full. This matters most for melodic sounds such as a bass.
- **Exactly when it starts.** Moving a note a tiny bit early or late is called **nudging**. A hi-hat that is slightly late sounds lazy and relaxed; a slightly early one sounds pushy and excited.
- **Its pitch.** You can move a note up or down without deleting it and placing it again.

The trick for all four edits is the same: **keep a step button held down**, then use another control. Holding the step tells Move which note you mean.

<Steps>
  <Step press="play" hear="Your beat from Lesson 9 plays round and round.">
    Press **Play** so you can hear every change as you make it.
    <Tip>No beat yet? Go back to [Lesson 9](/first-moves/lessons/09-first-beat/) and put a kick, a snare and a hi-hat on a few steps first. If the drum track now shows a newer clip, such as your capture from Lesson 12, press **Note/Session**, press the pad of your Lesson 9 clip in the drum row, then press that row's track button to come back to Note Mode.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="drum-pads" see="The step buttons now show only this drum sound's hits.">
    Make sure you're on your drum track, then tap the **hi-hat pad** in the left block. On a drum track, the step buttons light up only for the pad you picked last.
    <Tip>Can't remember which pad is the hi-hat? Tap the pads one by one and listen for a short, bright tick. If you are on the wrong track, press the track button for your drums first.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="step-3 volume" hold="step-3" hear="The hi-hat on step 3 changes in loudness while the others stay the same.">
    Hold down **Step 3**. If your hi-hats sit on other steps, hold any lit one instead. Keep holding it and turn the **Volume knob** a little. Try one direction, listen, then the other, and stop when that hit stands out.
    <Tip>Keep the step held the whole time. A quick tap on a lit step removes its note instead. If a hit disappears, press **Undo** to bring it back.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="step-7 right" hold="step-7" hear="The hi-hat on step 7 now lands a hair later than before.">
    Hold **Step 7** (or another lit hi-hat step) and press the **Right arrow** twice. Each press moves the note a tenth of a step later.
    <Tip>The change is small on purpose, so listen closely. Press a few more times to hear it clearly, or press the **Left arrow** (still holding the step) to move it back. Holding the arrow down longer moves the note by a whole step.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="track-2" see="The second track is selected. In a new Set it holds a bass.">
    Press the **second track button** from the top to switch to a melodic sound. In a brand-new Set, this track always holds a bass.
    <Tip>If this track sounds like drums, you may be in a demo Set or have swapped the sound. Try the other track buttons until neighbouring pads play the same instrument at different pitches.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="shift step-14" hold="shift" see="The step buttons show an empty bar: this track now has a fresh clip to fill.">
    Hold **Shift** and press **Step 14**. This gets a new, empty clip ready on the bass track, so any bass clip already there, like your bassline from [Lesson 11](/first-moves/lessons/11-record-live/), stays safe.
  </Step>
  <Step press="pads step-1" hear="A bass note now plays at the start of every loop, along with your beat.">
    Press one **pad**, then **Step 1**. This puts that note on Step 1, just like the drum hits you placed in Lesson 9.
    <Tip>If you don't hear it, check that the music is still playing, and press **Play** if it stopped.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="pads step-9" hear="The same bass note now also plays halfway through the loop.">
    Press the same **pad** again, then **Step 9**.
  </Step>
  <Step press="step-1 wheel" hold="step-1" see="As the note grows, the step lights after Step 1 get brighter: the note now covers them too.">
    Hold **Step 1** and turn **the wheel** to the right. Each click adds a tenth of a step, so turn it well over ten clicks to make the note last several steps.
    <Tip>The note can't grow past the next step that holds the same note, here Step 9. To make a note shorter and snappier, hold its step and turn the wheel to the left.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="step-9 plus" hold="step-9" hear="The second bass note is now slightly higher than the first.">
    Hold **Step 9** and press **Plus** once. The note moves up by one **semitone**, the smallest step between two notes. Press **Plus** a couple more times and listen to how the bassline changes.
    <Tip>Keep the step held. Without it, **Plus** moves the whole set of pads up an octave instead. If a pitch sounds odd, hold the step and press **Minus** to bring it back down. To jump a whole [octave](/first-moves/glossary/#octave) at once, hold the step and keep **Plus** or **Minus** down for a moment instead of tapping it.</Tip>
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Try it yourself

- Go back to your drums, tap the hi-hat pad, and give Step 11 the same accent as Step 3. Then make the other hi-hats a little softer. Can you make the pattern sound like a real drummer?
- Nudge a different hi-hat a little early instead of late. Which one feels better with your beat?
- On the bass, hold a step and look at the pads: the notes on that step light up. While holding the step, press another pad to add a second note, then press the lit pad to take it away.

When you're done, press **Play** to stop.

## Good to know

- You can hold several steps at once for any of these edits, and all their notes change together. Press them down and keep them all held; a quick tap on a second step does something else (see the next note).
- To nudge in really tiny amounts, hold **Shift** as well as the step while you press an arrow.
- Since software 1.5 there's a quicker way to change a note's length: hold its step and briefly press a later step. The note then stretches towards the step you tapped.
