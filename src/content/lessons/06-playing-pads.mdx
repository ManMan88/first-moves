## The idea

The pads work a lot like a real instrument: how you hit them matters. Tap a pad gently and the sound is usually quiet. Strike it firmly and it usually gets loud. Musicians call this strength **[velocity](/first-moves/glossary/#velocity)**. It is what makes a drum groove feel alive instead of robotic: real drummers play some hits softer and some harder.

What the pads play depends on the sound on the track you picked.

- A **drum kit** (Move calls it a [Drum Rack](/first-moves/glossary/#drum-rack)) puts one sound on each pad. On a drum track you play the kit on the 16 pads on the left, the 4 by 4 block. One pad might be a kick drum, another a snare, another a hi-hat or a clap.
- A **melodic instrument**, like a bass, piano or synth, plays the same sound at different pitches. Each pad is a note, and all 32 pads are yours to play.

Many sounds also react to **pressure**. After a note starts, you can keep your finger on the pad and push harder. On sounds that support it, this reshapes the note while it rings. This is called [aftertouch](/first-moves/glossary/#aftertouch), and each pad feels your pressure on its own.

<Steps>
  <Step press="track-1" see="The top track is now selected, and in Note Mode the pads play its sound.">
    Press the **top track button**, on the left of the pads. In a brand-new Set, like the one from the last lesson, the top track always holds a drum kit.
    <Tip>If tapping a pad starts or stops a clip instead of playing a sound, you are in Session Mode. Press **Note/Session** to switch to Note Mode.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="drum-pads" hear="If this track is a drum kit, the pads play different kinds of hits: booms, snaps, ticks and claps.">
    Tap each of the 16 pads in the left block, one by one, and listen.
    <Tip>If every pad plays the same kind of sound, just higher or lower, this track is a melodic instrument. You may have opened a demo Set or swapped a sound: press the other track buttons and try again. If none of your four tracks sounds like drums, [Lesson 8](/first-moves/lessons/08-choosing-sounds/) shows you how to choose one.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="drum-pads" hear="Gentle taps sound quiet; firm hits sound loud and punchy.">
    Pick one drum pad. Tap it very gently a few times, then hit it harder.
    <Tip>Use the pad of your finger, not your nail. Try three levels: a light touch, a medium tap and a firm hit.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="shift step-10" hold="shift" see="Full Velocity is now on. Nothing plays yet; the change is in how the pads respond.">
    Hold **Shift** and press **Step 10**. This turns on **Full Velocity**.
    <Tip>Use this in Note Mode, where you play a single track. If nothing seems to change, press **Note/Session** to make sure you are in Note Mode, then try again.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="drum-pads" hear="Every hit now plays at full strength, however gently you tap.">
    Tap the same drum pad softly again.
    <Tip>Full Velocity is handy when you want every hit to be loud and even, for example a steady kick drum.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="shift step-10" hold="shift" see="Full Velocity is off again, so the pads follow your touch.">
    Hold **Shift** and press **Step 10** again to turn Full Velocity off.
    <Tip>The same combination switches it on and off. Tap a pad softly to check: if it still sounds loud, press the combination once more.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="track-2" see="The second track is now selected. In a new Set it holds a bass: a melodic sound.">
    Press the **second track button** from the top. Its pads play notes of a melody instead of drums.
    <Tip>If this track sounds like drums, try the other track buttons. When neighbouring pads sound like the same instrument at different pitches, you have found a melodic sound.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="pads" hear="The pads play different notes of the same instrument.">
    Play a few pads one after another, slowly, as if you were picking out a tune.
    <Tip>Some notes sound better together than others. [Lesson 7](/first-moves/lessons/07-scales-and-octaves/) explains how the notes are laid out on the pads.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="pads" hear="On some sounds the note changes while you press harder, and relaxes when you ease off.">
    Press one pad and keep your finger on it. While it sounds, push down harder, then ease off.
    <Tip>Not every sound responds to pressure. If nothing changes, this sound simply ignores it. Try the other melodic tracks, and choose a sound with a long, held note.</Tip>
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Try it yourself

- Go back to your drum track and play a simple beat: a kick on one pad, a snare on another, and taps on a hi-hat in between. Play the hi-hat softly and the snare hard, and hear how much more natural it feels.
- Play a drum sound as a melody. On the drum track, hold **Shift** and press **Step 8** to turn on the **16 Pitches** layout. Press a drum pad in the left block to choose its sound, then play the 16 pads on the right: each one plays that sound at a different pitch. Hold **Shift** and press **Step 8** again to turn it off.
- On a melodic track, hold two or three pads down at once and lean into each one with a different pressure.

## Good to know

- In the 16 Pitches layout, **Plus** and **Minus** move the pitches on the right-hand pads up or down an octave.
- Every pad senses pressure by itself. Hold down a chord, press harder with one finger than the others, and only that finger's note changes. The manual names the Drift and Wavetable instruments as the ones that react to pressure, so presets built on them are the best place to try it.
