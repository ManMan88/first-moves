## The idea

When you hold a chord on a piano, all its notes ring out at once. An **[arpeggiator](/first-moves/glossary/#arpeggiator)** does something different: it plays the notes of the chord you hold one after another, over and over, in time with the beat. The result is called an **arpeggio**. Hold three pads and you get a rippling pattern that would be hard to play by hand.

Move's arpeggiator has three styles:

- **Arp Up** climbs from the lowest note of your chord to the highest, then starts again.
- **Arp Down** goes the other way, from high to low.
- **Arp Random** picks the order by chance, so the pattern keeps changing.

The **rate** sets how fast the notes follow each other, for example one note every sixteenth of a bar, or one every eighth. A faster rate gives a busier, more excited pattern.

The same menu also has plain **Repeat**. Instead of stepping through a chord, it plays whatever you hold again and again at the chosen rate. On a drum kit this is perfect for the quick "tsk-tsk-tsk" hi-hat rolls you hear in hip hop and electronic music. The arpeggiator only works on melodic sounds; Repeat works on both melodic sounds and drums.

<Steps>
  <Step press="play" hear="Your loop plays: drums, bass and chords.">
    Press **Play** so your loop runs underneath. It's much more fun to play along with a beat.
  </Step>
  <Step press="track-3" see="The third track is selected.">
    Press the **third track button** from the top. In a brand-new Set it holds a pad, keys, plucked or rhythmic sound. In the Set from [Lesson 15](/first-moves/lessons/15-bassline-and-chords/), it holds your chords.
    <Tip>The arpeggiator needs a melodic sound. If the pads play different drum hits, pick another track button. Short, plucky sounds show off the arpeggio best.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="shift step-11" hold="shift" see="The Repeat menu opens on the display.">
    Hold **Shift** and press **Step 11**.
    <Tip>Keep Shift held down until you've pressed Step 11. If the display doesn't change, press the track button again to make sure you're in Note Mode, then retry.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="wheel" see="A list of repeat styles appears.">
    With the **Repeat** entry highlighted, press **the wheel**.
    <Tip>If a different entry is highlighted, turn the wheel until **Repeat** is, then press.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="wheel" see="Arp Up is now the chosen style.">
    Turn **the wheel** to **Arp Up**, then press **the wheel** to confirm.
  </Step>
  <Step press="pad-4-1 pad-4-3 pad-4-5" hold="pad-4-1 pad-4-3 pad-4-5" hear="The three notes play one at a time, rising, again and again for as long as you hold them.">
    Hold a chord: in one row, press and hold the 1st, 3rd and 5th pads, the skip-one shape from [Lesson 15](/first-moves/lessons/15-bassline-and-chords/).
    <Tip>Hearing one note repeat instead of a pattern? Only one pad is down. Press all three together and keep holding them. If the chord just rings out with no pattern, go back two steps and check that **Arp Up** is chosen.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="wheel" see="The Rate value changes on the display.">
    Turn **the wheel** to the **Rate** entry and change the rate, then hold your chord again to hear the difference.
    <Tip>If turning the wheel only moves between entries, press **the wheel** on **Rate** first, then turn it.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="shift step-11" hold="shift" see="The Repeat menu closes.">
    Hold **Shift** and press **Step 11** again to leave the menu.
    <Tip>Keep **Shift** held until Step 11 is down. If a note appeared on Step 11 by mistake, press **Undo**.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="track-1" see="The top track, your drum kit, is selected.">
    Press the **top track button**. In a brand-new Set, it holds a drum kit. Tap the pads in the left block one by one and find the hi-hat by ear: a short, bright tick. Remember which pad it is.
    <Tip>If the pads play notes instead of drum hits, try the other track buttons until you find your drums.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="shift step-11" hold="shift" see="The Repeat menu opens, this time for the drum kit.">
    Hold **Shift** and press **Step 11**.
  </Step>
  <Step press="drum-pads" hold="drum-pads" hear="A fast, even roll of hits for as long as you hold the pad.">
    Press and hold the hi-hat pad you found. Let go to stop the roll.
    <Tip>Turn **the wheel** while the menu is open to pick a faster or slower rate. Slow rates sound like steady eighth notes; fast ones sound like a buzz.</Tip>
  </Step>
</Steps>

When you're done, hold **Shift** and press **Step 11** to close the menu, then press **Play** to stop.

## Try it yourself

- Go back to your chord track and try **Arp Down** and **Arp Random**. Which one suits your beat best?
- Hold two pads, then three, then four. The arpeggio grows with every note you add.
- Play a hi-hat roll only at the end of each bar, as a small fill. Now press **Record** and play it for real: repeated notes you record end up on the step buttons like any other notes.

## Good to know

- An arpeggio on a single held note simply repeats it at the chosen rate.
- On a melodic track you can also pick the plain **Repeat** style instead of an Arp style. Then every note you hold, alone or in a chord, repeats at the rate you set.
