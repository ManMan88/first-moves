## The idea

The **[tempo](/first-moves/glossary/#tempo)** is how fast the music goes. It's measured in **[BPM](/first-moves/glossary/#bpm)**, short for *beats per minute*: how many beats fit into one minute. A resting heartbeat is around 60 to 80 BPM, which suits a slow, calm song. A brisk walk is closer to 120, a common speed for pop and house music. Fast drum and bass runs at around 170. Changing the tempo speeds up or slows down everything in your Set together, but the notes stay where you put them.

A **[metronome](/first-moves/glossary/#metronome)** is a steady click that marks the beats. Musicians use it to stay in time, especially when they record. It's there to help you keep time, not to be part of the beat.

**[Groove](/first-moves/glossary/#groove)** changes how the music *feels*. A beat played by a computer can sound stiff, because every hit lands exactly on the grid. Move's groove is a **swing**: it takes every second step and plays it a little late. Short, even notes become a long-short, long-short pattern, like the bounce in a shuffle or a hip-hop beat. You can hear swing best on sounds that play on the in-between steps, so first you'll add a few hi-hats there.

    When you like the speed, press **the wheel** to confirm it.
  </Step>
  <Step press="shift step-7" hold="shift" see="The display switches to the Groove setting, ready to change.">
    Hold **Shift** and press **Step 7** to open the Groove setting.
  </Step>
  <Step press="wheel" hear="The hi-hats start to bounce: long, short, long, short.">
    Turn **the wheel** slowly to raise the groove amount, and listen to the hi-hats. At 0% there's no swing at all. At 100% you get a full, classic shuffle swing. Going beyond 100% makes the effect even stronger.
    <Tip>Hear no change? Swing only moves notes on even-numbered steps. Check that your hi-hats are on Steps 4, 8, 12 and 16, as you set them up at the start of this lesson.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="wheel" see="The groove screen closes. While a groove is on, its icon stays visible under Step 7.">
    Pick an amount you like and press **the wheel** to confirm it. To compare with and without, open the groove again later and turn it back to 0%.
  </Step>
  <Step press="shift step-6" hold="shift" hear="A steady click on top of your music.">
    Hold **Shift** and press **Step 6**. This opens the Metronome setting and switches the click on. Count along with the clicks: *one, two, three, four*.
    <Tip>While the click is switched on, a small metronome icon shows under **Step 6**. That's a quick way to check whether it's on.</Tip>
  </Step>

  </Step>

When you're done, press **Play** to stop the music.

## Try it yourself

Play with speed and feel together:

- Set your beat to around 90 BPM with lots of swing, then to around 128 BPM with none. The same notes can sound like two different styles of music.
- Switch the metronome on, and play the snare pad along with the click, trying to land right on the beat.
- Find the swing amount where the hi-hats sound relaxed but not sloppy. Every beat has its own sweet spot.

## Good to know

- Tempo and groove belong to the whole Set: every track follows the same speed and the same swing.
- The groove only changes when notes play, not where they sit. Your steps stay exactly where you placed them.
- The metronome is especially handy when you record yourself playing, which you'll do in [Lesson 11](/first-moves/lessons/11-record-live/).
