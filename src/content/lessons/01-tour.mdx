## The idea

Move is a complete little music studio in one box. It has its own battery, its own speaker and its own sounds, so you don't need a computer, a phone or cables to make music with it. Everything you make lives on Move itself.

All that power sits behind a lot of buttons. Don't worry about what each one does yet. In this lesson you just learn **where** things are, the way you'd learn where the pedals are before driving a car. Later lessons use each group for real.

For each step, the drawing lights up the part to find. Find it on your Move and rest a finger on it. You don't need to press anything, and nothing needs to happen. If your Move is already switched on, a few steps show you a small bonus you can notice.

<Steps view="both">
  <Step press="pads" see="Four rows of eight soft, square pads: 32 in total.">
    Find the big block of **pads** in the middle. This is where you play: drums, notes and chords. The pads feel how hard you hit them, so a soft tap plays quietly and a firm hit plays loudly.
  </Step>
  <Step press="steps" see="A row of 16 small buttons, with a short line under Steps 1, 5, 9 and 13.">
    Find the long row of 16 **step buttons**. Each one stands for a small slice of time in a pattern. The little lines under Steps 1, 5, 9 and 13 mark where each beat begins, so the row splits into four beats of four steps.
    <Tip summary="What's a beat?">A beat is the steady pulse you'd tap your foot to. You'll use the step buttons to build beats in a later lesson.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="tracks" see="Four buttons just to the left of the pads.">
    Find the four **track buttons** just to the left of the pads. A song on Move has four tracks, like four band members, and these buttons choose which one you're playing.
  </Step>
  <Step press="encoders volume" see="Eight knobs in a row, plus one more knob on its own in the top right corner.">
    Find the eight **knobs** above the pads, then the **Volume knob** in the top right corner. The eight knobs shape the sound (the manual calls them [encoders](/first-moves/glossary/#encoder)). The Volume knob sets how loud Move is. Just touch the knobs for now; don't turn them.
    <Tip summary="Is Move switched on?">The knobs sense your touch. If Move is on, resting a finger on one shows on the display which setting it controls. Turning is what changes a setting, so keep your fingers still.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="display wheel" see="A small screen, and a round wheel that turns and can be pressed down like a button.">
    Find the **display**, then the **wheel**. The display tells you what's going on. The wheel is how you move through lists and menus: turn it to scroll, press it to choose.
  </Step>
  <Step press="shift" hold="shift" see="If Move is on, small icons appear under several of the step buttons while you hold Shift.">
    Find **Shift** and hold it down for a moment. Shift gives many buttons a second job. While you hold it, small icons show up beneath several step buttons. Each icon marks a menu or feature you can open by holding **Shift** and pressing that step. You'll use the one under Step 2 in the next lesson.
  </Step>
  <Step press="back note-session" see="You've found the two buttons, one for going back and one for switching views.">
    Find **Back** and **Note/Session**. **Back** gets you out of menus and lists, so it's your escape hatch when you feel lost. **Note/Session** flips Move between its two main views: one for building a single track, and one for seeing your whole song at once.
  </Step>
  <Step press="play record" see="You've found the Play button and the Record button.">
    Find **Play** and **Record**, but don't press them yet. **Play** starts and stops your music. **Record** captures what you play so Move can repeat it.
  </Step>
  <Step press="capture sampling loop mute delete copy undo" see="You've found all seven function buttons.">
    Find the function buttons, one by one, as the drawing numbers them: **Capture**, **Sampling**, **Loop**, **Mute**, **Delete**, **Copy** and **Undo**. The numbers only help you find them; you don't need to press any of these.
    <Tip summary="Worried about pressing the wrong thing?">**Undo** takes back your last action, so most mistakes are easy to fix. Knowing where it is makes the rest of the course less scary.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="left right plus minus" see="You've found two arrow buttons, a plus button and a minus button.">
    Find the **Left arrow**, **Right arrow**, **Plus (+)** and **Minus (−)** buttons. The arrows step through the sections of a longer pattern. Plus and minus shift the pads to higher or lower notes when you play a melody.
  </Step>
  <Step press="power audio-out audio-in usb-a usb-c" see="Five things on the back edge: the yellow power button and four sockets.">
    Turn Move around and look at the back edge. Find the yellow **Power button**, the **Audio out** socket for headphones or speakers, the **Audio in** socket for recording another sound source, the **USB-A** port for a MIDI keyboard, and the **USB-C** port for charging.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Try it yourself

Close your eyes (or look away from the screen) and try to point to each of these on your Move: **Shift**, **Back**, **Undo**, the **Volume knob**, the **wheel** and the **power button**. Those six are the ones you'll reach for most in the next few lessons.

## Good to know

- Move also has a built-in microphone and speaker, so it can record and play sound with nothing plugged in.
- You don't need to memorise the whole panel. The [controls page](/first-moves/controls/) lets you tap any part of Move to see what it does, whenever you need a reminder.
