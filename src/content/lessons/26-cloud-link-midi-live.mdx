## The idea

Everything in this course so far happened inside Move. That's the point of it: you can pick it up and make music anywhere. But Move can also talk to the world around it, and this last lesson is a short tour of the four main ways.

- **Ableton Cloud** keeps copies of up to eight Sets online, so you can carry on with a song in the Note app on a phone or tablet, in Ableton Live on a computer, or on Push. You switch it on once in Move Manager ([Lesson 25](/first-moves/lessons/25-move-manager/)), using an Ableton account.
- **Link** keeps Move in time with other apps and devices on the same network. Start a drum loop on Move, and a friend's app on a phone plays along at the same speed.
- **MIDI** is a common language music gear uses to send notes. It carries *which* note was played and how hard, not the sound itself. With it, a USB keyboard can play Move's sounds.
- **Control Live Mode** turns Move into a controller for Ableton Live on a computer.

You don't need any of these to make music on Move. Try the ones that sound useful, and skip the rest.

  <Step press="shift step-1" hold="shift" see="The Set Overview, with a lit pad for each of your Sets.">
    Let's start with Cloud. Hold **Shift** and press **Step 1** to see your Sets.
  </Step>
  <Step press="shift pads" hold="shift" see="The display shows the options for that Set.">
    Hold **Shift** and press the pad of a Set you'd like to take elsewhere. Let go of both.
    <Tip summary="Only see Cloud Off?">Cloud isn't switched on yet. Open that entry to see the address for Move Manager, then in Move Manager go to the **Settings** page and click **Activate Cloud**. You'll sign in to your Ableton account there. [Lesson 25](/first-moves/lessons/25-move-manager/) shows how to open Move Manager.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="wheel" see="The entry briefly shows that the Set is uploading, then a message says it's done.">
    Turn **the wheel** to **Upload to Cloud** and press the wheel. After the upload, the Set's icon on the display gets a small cloud.
    <Tip>Uploading needs Wi-Fi. No connection right now? Don't worry: Move holds on to the upload and sends it when Wi-Fi returns. If Move says the limit is reached, you already have eight Sets in Cloud: make one of them local first (see Good to know).</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="shift step-2" hold="shift" see="The Setup menu opens.">
    Now Link. Hold **Shift** and press **Step 2**.
  </Step>
  <Step press="wheel" see="The Link entry opens, with three choices.">
    Turn **the wheel** to **Link** and press the wheel to open it. You'll find three choices:
    <Screen lines={['Off', 'Tempo', 'Start/Stop']} />
    **Tempo** makes Move follow the shared speed. **Start/Stop** does that too, and also starts and stops Move together with the others. **Off** means Move keeps its own time.
  </Step>
  <Step press="wheel" see="Tempo is chosen, and Move now follows the speed of any Link session it finds.">
    Turn **the wheel** to **Tempo**.
    <Tip>If the choice doesn't seem to stick, press the wheel once while Tempo is highlighted. Link only finds apps and devices on the same network as Move. If Move is plugged into a computer by USB-C, the computer's network works too. While Link is on, recording has no count-in, so choose **Off** again when you're playing alone.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="back" hold="back" see="The Setup menu closes.">
    Hold **Back** to leave the Setup menu.
  </Step>
  <Step press="usb-a" see="Nothing changes yet: Move is ready to listen to the keyboard.">
    Now MIDI. Plug a USB MIDI keyboard into the **USB-A** port on Move's back. Move can power a small keyboard through the same cable.
    <Tip>Most small USB keyboards work, as long as they need no special driver on a computer. Very fast USB gear, such as Push, won't work here.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="tracks" hear="Playing the keyboard plays the sound of the track you picked.">
    Press a **track button** to open a track, then play some keys on your keyboard. To play a different sound, press a different track button.
    <Tip>If nothing sounds, hold **Shift** and press that same track button. This opens a menu of options for that track. Find the **MIDI In** setting with **the wheel** and set it to **Auto** (see the note below). Then press **Back** to leave the menu.</Tip>
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note kind="newer" firmware="1.5">
  Since Move 1.5, MIDI can come in and go out at the same time, and each track has its own **MIDI In** and **MIDI Out** channel. You set them in the menu you open by holding **Shift** and pressing a track button. A MIDI cable can carry 16 separate channels, like lanes on a road. On **Auto**, the track you have selected takes every channel that no other track has claimed, so whichever track you pick, your keyboard plays it.
</Note>

## Try it yourself

- Put a Set in Cloud, then open the Note app on a phone or tablet signed in to the same Ableton account and find your Set there.
- Turn on Link with **Tempo**, open any Link-enabled app on a phone on the same Wi-Fi, and change the tempo in the app. Watch Move follow.
- With a keyboard plugged in, record a melody from it into a clip, just like playing the pads ([Lesson 11](/first-moves/lessons/11-record-live/)).

## Good to know

- **Taking a Set out of Cloud.** Hold **Shift**, press the Set's pad, and choose **Make Local**. Move keeps its copy; only the online one goes.
- **Note Sets on Move.** A Note Set can have lots of tracks, but Move only has room for four. You'll get the first four, and the rest stay behind in Note.
- **Control Live Mode** needs Ableton Live (12.1 or newer, or 11.3.35 or newer) on a computer and a USB-C cable. It's the **Control Live** entry in the Setup menu. To come back, open Setup again and choose the **Standalone** entry, which takes its place. While you're in Control Live Mode, a keyboard on the USB-A port won't work.
- **Headphones and speakers.** The audio out jack on the back works for both. The audio in jack takes line-level sound, for example from a phone; a microphone or a guitar needs a preamp in between.
- **Move as a sound card.** Plugged into a computer by USB-C, Move can send its audio to the computer, to record it in Live or another app. Choose what it sends in the **USB-C Audio** entry in Setup. With **Main Out** chosen, Move's own speaker goes quiet.

<Note kind="newer" firmware="1.7">
  Since Move 1.7, MIDI also works over the **USB-C** port: a computer, an iPad or iPhone, or Push 3 can send notes to Move and receive them from it. This only works in Standalone Mode, Move's normal mode.
</Note>

<Note kind="newer" firmware="2.0">
  **Link Audio** streams sound, not just timing, between Link devices. Move can send its main output or single tracks to audio tracks in Live. Ableton recommends connecting by USB-C for the most reliable stream.
</Note>

## Where to go next

You've reached the end of First Moves. Well done! Here's where to keep going:

- **The official [Move manual](https://www.ableton.com/en/move/manual/)** covers every feature in depth. Now that you know your way around, it will read much more easily.
- **[Learning Music](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/)** is a free, playful site from Ableton about beats, melodies, chords and song structure. Everything you try there, you can try on Move.
- **[Learning Synths](https://learningsynths.ableton.com/)** explains how synthesizers make sound, one knob at a time. It's the perfect next step after [Lesson 17](/first-moves/lessons/17-shape-the-sound/).

Most of all: keep making things. Start a new Set, grab a sound you like, and see where it goes.
