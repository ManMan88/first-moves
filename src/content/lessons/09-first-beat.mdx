## The idea

Put on almost any song and you'll find yourself nodding or tapping your foot. Each tap lands on a **[beat](/first-moves/glossary/#beat)**: the steady pulse that holds the music together. Beats come in groups, and most music counts them in fours: *one, two, three, four, one, two, three, four*. Each group of four beats is called a **[bar](/first-moves/glossary/#bar)**.

The row of 16 buttons along the bottom of Move are the **step buttons**. Together they show one bar. With the normal settings, every beat is split into 4 short slots called **[steps](/first-moves/glossary/#step)** (musicians call them 16th notes, because there are 16 in a bar). So 4 beats of 4 steps fill all 16 buttons. Look closely and you'll see a small line under the lights of Steps 1, 5, 9 and 13: those are the four places where a new beat begins.

To make a beat, you choose a drum sound on a pad and then switch on the steps where it should play. Move loops the bar round and round and plays each sound at exactly the right moment. This way of building music, placing notes on a grid instead of playing them live, is called **[sequencing](/first-moves/glossary/#sequencing)**.

The most famous dance beat puts a kick drum on every beat. It's called *four on the floor*:

<StepGrid on={[1, 5, 9, 13]} caption="Four on the floor: a kick drum on the first step of each beat." />

Then a snare or clap answers on beats 2 and 4. That's the "backbeat" you clap along to in most pop and rock songs:

<StepGrid on={[5, 13]} caption="The backbeat: a snare or clap on beats 2 and 4, which are steps 5 and 13." />

<Steps>
  <Step press="track-1" see="The top track is selected. Its pads play drum sounds.">
    Press the **top track button**, on the left of the pads. In a brand-new Set this track always holds a drum kit.
    <Tip>If the pads start or stop clips, press **Note/Session** to get to Note Mode. If this track doesn't sound like drums (maybe you swapped its sound in [Lesson 8](/first-moves/lessons/08-choosing-sounds/)), make a fresh Set as in [Lesson 5](/first-moves/lessons/05-new-set/).</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="drum-pads" hear="A mix of drum hits. The kick is a deep, round boom.">
    Tap the 16 pads in the left block one by one. Listen for the **kick drum**: the lowest, deepest thump in the kit. Finish by tapping the kick pad once more, so it's the one Move has selected.
    <Tip>Every kit is different, so there's no fixed "kick pad". Trust your ears. If two pads both sound deep, pick the one you like best.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="step-1" hear="The kick plays once per bar, over and over.">
    Press **Step 1**. The step turns white, which means it now holds a note. Move starts playing by itself, and a green light walks along the step buttons to show where the music is.
    <Tip>If nothing starts playing, press **Play**. Move only starts on its own when the first note goes into an empty clip, so a track that already had a clip needs a press of Play.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="step-5 step-9 step-13" hear="A steady kick on every beat: four on the floor.">
    Press **Step 5**, **Step 9** and **Step 13**. Now the kick lands at the start of every beat.
    <Tip>Pressed the wrong step? Press it again briefly and the note disappears.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="drum-pads" hear="A sharp crack or a clap on top of the kick.">
    While the beat plays, tap the other drum pads to find a **snare** or **clap**: a bright, snappy hit. Tap your favourite one last to select it.
    <Tip>The step lights change when you select a different pad. They only show the notes of the selected pad, so the kick seems to vanish. Don't worry: it's still there and still playing.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="step-5 step-13" hear="The kick on every beat, and the snare answering on beats 2 and 4.">
    Press **Step 5** and **Step 13** to put the snare on beats 2 and 4.
    <Tip>These steps look empty before you press them, even though the kick plays there. That's because only the snare's notes are shown now. Two sounds can share a step without any problem.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="drum-pads" hear="A short, bright tick or tss: a hi-hat.">
    Now find a **hi-hat**: a short, crisp *tss* or *tick*. Tap it last so it's selected.
  </Step>
  <Step press="step-3 step-7 step-11 step-15" hear="A hi-hat halfway between each beat, filling the gaps between the kicks and snares.">
    Press **Step 3**, **Step 7**, **Step 11** and **Step 15**. These sit exactly halfway between the beats, on the **off-beats**.
  </Step>
  <Step press="step-15" hear="The last hi-hat of the bar is gone.">
    To take a hit away, make sure the hi-hat pad is still selected, then give **Step 15** a quick, short press. Its light goes dim again.
    <Tip>If a note appears instead of disappearing, a different pad was selected. Press the step again to remove that new note, tap the hi-hat pad, and try once more. Keep the press short: holding a step down is for editing its note, which comes later in the course.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="play" hear="Silence.">
    Press **Play** to stop. Press it again whenever you want to hear your beat once more.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Try it yourself

Make the beat your own:

- Put the hi-hat back on Step 15, then try hi-hats on **every** step, or every other step, and hear how much busier the beat feels.
- Add an extra kick on Step 16 or Step 11, just before a beat. Little surprises like this give a beat its bounce.
- Find another sound in the kit, like a tom or a shaker, and give it a pattern of its own.

## Good to know

- You can also do it the other way round: hold a step button down and then tap a pad.
- The 16 steps show one bar. To make longer patterns that change from bar to bar, you'll learn about bars and loop length later in the course. The **Left arrow** and **Right arrow** buttons move between bars.
- Each step is a 16th note only with the normal setting. Workflow Settings has a Step Grid entry that can make the steps longer or shorter. Leave it alone for now.
