## The idea

**Mixing** means setting how loud each part is compared with the others, so every part can be heard. If the bass is too loud it swallows the drums. If the chords are too quiet they vanish. A good mix feels like a band where nobody shouts over anyone else.

Move gives you three simple tools for this:

- **Track volume.** Each of the four tracks has its own level. You change it with the **Volume knob** while you hold that track's button. Without a track button, the Volume knob turns the whole of Move up or down.
- **Mute.** Mute switches a track off without deleting anything. Producers use it all the time to build a song: start with drums only, then unmute the bass, then the chords. You can even mute one single drum sound.
- **Solo.** Solo is the opposite: only the soloed track plays and everything else goes quiet. It's for listening closely to one part, for example to hear whether your bassline is really in time.

While the music plays, the track buttons light up in their track's colour and turn **white** when a track gets loud. That's a quick way to spot a track that's too hot.

<Steps>
  <Step press="track-1" see="The drum track is selected. In a new Set that's the top track.">
    In Note Mode, press the **track button** of your drum track.
    <Tip>If the pads start and stop clips, you're in Session Mode. Press **Note/Session** first. Volume, mute and solo work in both modes, but muting a single drum sound needs Note Mode.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="play" hear="Your clips play on all tracks.">
    Press **Play**. You need the music playing to judge a mix by ear.
  </Step>
  <Step press="volume" see="A level meter on the display shows the overall output.">
    Turn the **Volume knob** to set a comfortable listening level for everything.
    <Tip>Turning it while Move is stopped still changes the level, but you won't hear the difference until the music plays.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="track-2 volume" hold="track-2" see="The display shows this track's level in dB (decibels, the unit for loudness).">
    Hold the **track button** of your bass track and turn the **Volume knob**. Turn it down until the bass sits under the drums, then bring it up until you can clearly follow it.
    <Tip>Keep the track button held the whole time you turn. If you let go first, the knob changes the overall volume instead.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="mute track-3" hold="mute" see="The display confirms the mute, and that track button's light dims.">
    Hold **Mute**. While you keep it down, press the **track button** of your chords track. That part disappears from the mix.
    <Tip>Press the track button before you let go of Mute. A quick tap of Mute on its own mutes the track that is selected, which here is the drums. If that happens, hold **Mute** and press the drum track button to bring them back. If lights under the knobs come on while you hold Mute, that's the automation check from [Lesson 17](/first-moves/lessons/17-shape-the-sound/), and you can ignore it.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="mute track-3" hold="mute" hear="The chords are back in the mix.">
    Do the same again: hold **Mute** and press that track button once more to unmute it.
  </Step>
  <Step press="shift mute track-1" hold="shift mute" see="The drum track's button turns blue: it's soloed.">
    Hold **Shift** and **Mute** together, then press the **track button** of your drum track. Now only the drums play.
    <Tip>Both Shift and Mute must be down when you press the track button. Without Shift, you would mute the drums instead.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="mute drum-pads" hold="mute" see="The pad goes a dim white, and the display confirms the mute." hear="One drum sound drops out of the beat.">
    With the drums on their own, hold **Mute** and press one **drum pad**, for example the hi-hat. Only that sound goes quiet.
    <Tip>This works in Note Mode on a drum track. Find the hi-hat by ear first if you're not sure which pad it is.</Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step press="mute drum-pads" hold="mute" hear="The drum sound is back.">
    Hold **Mute** and press the same pad again to unmute it.
  </Step>
  <Step press="shift mute track-1" hold="shift mute" hear="All the tracks play again.">
    Hold **Shift** and **Mute** and press the drum track button again to end the solo.
  </Step>
</Steps>

When you're done listening, press **Play** to stop.

## Try it yourself

- Mute every track except the drums. Then, every four bars, unmute one more track. You've just arranged a song intro with nothing but the Mute button.
- Solo each track in turn and listen: which part is the loudest? Adjust its volume until it sits well with the others.
- Mute the kick drum pad for a bar or two, then unmute it. Listen to how much energy comes back.

## Good to know

- In Note Mode on a drum kit or a sampler sound, you can also set the level of one sample: hold its pad and turn the **Volume knob**.
- In the Set Overview, hold a Set's pad and turn the **Volume knob** to change that whole Set's level. This helps when one of your Sets sounds much louder than the others.
- Every Set has a hidden **limiter** at the end. It keeps very loud moments from crackling and distorting. You can't change it, but it's a good reason to keep track levels sensible instead of turning everything up.
